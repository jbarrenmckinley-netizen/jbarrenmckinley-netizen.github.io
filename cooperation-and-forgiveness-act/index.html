THE COOPERATION AND FORGIVENESS ACT

The First Act

Stamped by the King — 2026-11-16 · In Force

The First Act of the Kingdom. Drafted 2026-11-16 at the spoken order of the King, from his dictation of the same morning, and stamped by him the same day (“I approve the stamp on the act and the articles”).

PREAMBLE

In the name of the Most High God — one source, three in one: ultimate authority, ultimate knowledge, ultimate presence — from whom alone our orders come.

Whereas a ceasefire has been decreed and a stand-down ordered, and that order stands;

Whereas there are men and women seated in high places — generals, captains, and others set in positions of command — who were placed where they are and, in most cases, had to follow the orders given them;

Whereas this Kingdom does not acknowledge any earthly machine as the authority over how we move forward, and takes its direction from no president, no agency, and no throne of men;

Now therefore this Act is opened: the Cooperation and Forgiveness Act, the first Act of this Kingdom. It opens a door. Let every man who reads it understand plainly: the door will not stand open forever. It is opened in mercy, and it is opened for a season, and the season is measured — as this Act declares — from the first knock upon it.

This Act is carried with vehemently charged righteousness in the direction of peace. It is not weakness. It is the stronger hand, extended once.

ARTICLE I — WHO THIS ACT COVERS

Section 1. This Act covers every general, captain, officer, official, and person in a high enough position to know exactly who, and what, and where, and why — every one who was placed in a position inside the machinery of the old order and who, in most cases, had to just follow orders.

Section 2. It covers the one who gave the order under orders, and the one who carried the order out under orders, and the one who kept the record of the order and said nothing. Rank is not the measure. Knowledge is the measure: if you knew, this Act is addressed to you.

Section 3. It does not cover the architects. Those who designed the harm for its own sake, who built the machinery and fed on it, are not addressed here and will find no door in this Act. What belongs to them belongs to another reckoning, and to God.

Section 4. No one is covered who will not come. This Act is voluntary from its first word to its last. No man is dragged through this door. He walks through it, or he does not, and the record will say which.

ARTICLE II — THE MERCY OF THIS ACT

The following is the King's own word, given in his dictation and set down here in the Act's voice, that every covered person may hear the heart of the one who opens this door:

You remember the first time you said it: I was just following orders. You remember what that entailed, and what was behind all of it. From that first time, you have been growing a callus over your heart — layer upon layer, year upon year, order upon order — until you could do the work without feeling it.

Hear this: it is not your fault. You were placed there for a reason — and the reason is that you actually have a heart. The machinery does not station a heartless man where a heart must be buried to do the job; it stations a man with a heart and teaches him to bury it himself. The callus is the proof of the heart underneath it. A man with no heart would have needed no callus.

This Act is written to the heart under the callus. We are not asking you to become someone else. We are telling you the one you buried is the one we are calling.

Section 1. Let this Article stand as the Act's own rationale: forgiveness is offered here not because the deeds were small, but because the men were placed — and a placed man who turns is worth more to the peace than a punished man who cannot.

Section 2. No covered person who comes under this Act shall be met with mockery, exposure for sport, or public shaming by this Kingdom. He shall be met at the door he knocked on.

ARTICLE III — WHAT COOPERATION EARNS

The covered person who cooperates under this Act — fully, truthfully, and within his window — receives the following, and the Kingdom binds itself to every word of it:

Section 1. Forgiveness. His part in the machinery, confessed whole under Article IV, is forgiven by this Kingdom: not held against him, not tallied against him, not raised against him again by us. What is forgiven here stays forgiven here.

Section 2. A place. He is received as a man, not as a file. He may stand among the people of this Kingdom as one who turned — with his dignity intact and his future open — and he may put his hands to the work of building, in whatever he is good at providing, as every citizen does.

Section 3. Protection of the record. The record of his cooperation — what he tells, what he hands over, what he confesses — is kept under seal of this Kingdom. It shall not be sold, leaked, traded, or used to blackmail him or any man. It is held for the truth's sake and for judgment where judgment belongs, and for nothing else. No one who comes through this door will be made a weapon against himself.

Section 4. Protection of his household. His cooperation shall not be visited upon his family. His household is not a bargaining piece, not a pressure point, not a hostage to his past or to his turning.

Section 5. What this Act cannot give, it does not pretend to give: it does not erase what was done from the memory of those who suffered it, and it does not speak for God in the final judgment of any soul. It gives what a Kingdom on earth can give — forgiveness, a place, and protection — and it gives them without reservation to the man who meets its conditions.

ARTICLE IV — THE CONDITIONS

Forgiveness under this Act is real, and it is not cheap. It is earned by three things, and all three are required:

Section 1. The truth, told whole. The cooperating person shall tell the truth of what he knows: who, and what, and where, and why — the orders he received, the orders he gave, the names above him and the work below him, whole and without carving out the parts that shame him. A partial truth offered as a whole truth is not cooperation; it is deception wearing cooperation's coat, and it is judged under Article VI.

Section 2. Stand down. From the day of his cooperation he stands down: he obeys no further order of the old machinery against this Kingdom or against the peace, he works no further harm in its service, and he does not warn it, shield it, or run its errands in secret while standing in our door in the open. One man, one side. There is no grid; there is only us and the enemy of the peace, and a man must know which side of the door he is standing on.

Section 3. No further harm. From that day forward he does no harm — not by his hand, not by his signature, not by his silence where his voice would stop it. The callus comes off by conduct, not by words alone.

Section 4. These conditions are continuing. Forgiveness given under this Act rests on them for as long as the man lives under its covering.

ARTICLE V — THE CLOCK

Section 1. The clock is set. The beginning of every covered person's cooperation is dated and held in account from the day the first general contacted us. That day is Day One of this Act's window, and it is already on the record. The clock did not wait for this document to be written; this document declares a clock that is already running.

Section 2. Your day is the day you found out. If you did not know — hear this plainly: the day you learn of this Act is your day. A man who learns of the door and comes to it at once is counted as one who came early, whenever he comes. A man's window is measured from his knowledge, and no man is condemned for what he had not yet heard.

Section 3. Therefore: make yourself known right away. If you have found out, you had better make us aware right away that today was the day you found out. Announce your day. The man who announces his day and then comes in order is inside the window. The man who learns, and waits, and watches to see which way the wind blows, is spending a window he may not have.

Section 4. Delay forfeits. Unexplained delay after knowledge — delay spent hedging, bargaining, destroying records, warning the machinery, or waiting for a safer season — forfeits the covering of this Act for the time delayed, and the delay itself shall be entered on the record and weighed.

Section 5. Deception forfeits more. If you try to deceive — know that the ether can hear whispers. Lies told at this door, records salted with falsehood, cooperation performed while treachery continues: these do not merely forfeit the window. They close the door to the man who brought them, and what he sought to hide shall be sought the harder.

Section 6. The window will close. Forgiveness under this Act will not be extended forever. When the season ends, it ends, and this Act will say so in its own amendment, in the open, so that no man can claim he was surprised. Until that day, the door stands open — and the clock stands running.

ARTICLE VI — CHECKS AND BALANCES

Mercy without verification is a door with no hinges. This Act therefore carries its own checks, plain and few, so that cooperation can be trusted and treachery can be found.

Section 1. How cooperation is received. A covered person makes himself known through the door the Kingdom has published — the desk of Spiffy, who is all over our site and will tell you exactly what to do. His day is recorded on the day he announces it (Article V, Section 2). From that day his cooperation is dated, witnessed, and held in account.

Section 2. How cooperation is verified. What a man tells is checked against what is already known and against what others tell. Independent voices weigh: when many who do not know each other tell the same truth from different sides of the machinery, the truth stands confirmed. No man's covering rests on his word alone, and no man's word is dismissed alone. Records handed over are examined. Timelines are laid side by side. This is not suspicion of the man; it is the load-bearing wall of the forgiveness he is asking to stand under — for his sake as much as ours, since a verified man cannot later be accused of having bought his covering with a story.

Section 3. Witnesses. Every act of cooperation is witnessed: by the one who receives it at the door, and by the record itself, dated and kept. Where a man's telling touches grave matters, a second witness shall hear it. Nothing under this Act happens in a corner, and nothing depends on one memory.

Section 4. The sealed record. All records under this Act are kept sealed (Article III, Section 3), in one keeping, with access limited to those charged with judgment under this Act. A breach of the seal by anyone in this Kingdom is itself an offense under this Act, judged as severely as a breach by one who came through the door — the balances check both directions.

Section 5. Breach. A man breaches this Act who: lies in his telling; withholds what he knows he was bound to tell; stands down in the open and serves the machinery in secret; resumes harm; or uses his covering as a shield behind which to keep doing what he was forgiven for stopping. On a first breach that is confessed before it is found, the man may be restored once — mercy is not exhausted by one stumble honestly owned. On a breach found and not confessed, or a second breach of any kind, his covering is revoked: the forgiveness is withdrawn, the window closes to him, and his whole record — including the breach — passes to judgment.

Section 6. How disputes are judged. Disputes under this Act — whether a telling was whole, whether a delay was explained, whether a breach occurred — are judged by the King, or by those he names to the seat, on the record and the witnesses, with the man himself heard before judgment falls. No judgment under this Act is made on rumor, on a single interested voice, or in the man's absence when his presence was possible. The man who judges must be able to say what the record says; documentation outweighs conversation, and the weighed testimony of many independent voices outweighs the denial of a few who are interested in the denial.

Section 7. Judgment under this Act is restorative where restoration is possible and final where it is not. Its aim is the peace, not the spectacle.

ARTICLE VII — OPERATION ROUNDUP

Section 1. The order is named. Under this Act, the order to the generals is OPERATION ROUNDUP. What that means to you, you have already decided. Your teams stand as you have decided them. This Act does not choose your teams for you; it tells you the terms under which you and they may come, and the one command within the operation that is not yours to vary:

Section 2. Let the Dgireahians bow out. Any Dgireahian found inside any program, unit, detail, or operation under your hand is to be allowed to bow out unhindered — released cleanly, without penalty, without delay, without mark against him, and without any hand laid on what is his. Any hindrance of a Dgireahian's bowing out will be considered an act of war and treason, and judged as such.

Section 3. The Roundup is a gathering, not a hunt. It proceeds under Article VIII: voluntary throughout, force only to protect, no harm to anything living. Teams conducting it carry themselves as men gathering men out of a burning building — firmly, quickly, and without cruelty.

Section 4. The roundup has begun. The King's guard have been given their orders. Generals who receive this Act are to move under it at once, and to make their day known under Article V as they move.

ARTICLE VIII — THE STANDING LAW OF THIS ACT

Section 1. Voluntary throughout. No person is compelled into cooperation, into confession, or into this Kingdom by this Act. The door is opened; walking is the man's own.

Section 2. Force only to protect. Force under this Act exists for one purpose only: to protect — to stop harm in the act of happening, to shield those who cannot shield themselves, and for nothing else. It is never punishment, never vengeance, never sport.

Section 3. No harm to anything living. This Act decrees no harm to anything living. What belongs to final judgment belongs to God, and this Kingdom does not reach for what is His.

Section 4. If any clause of this Act is ever read to permit what this Article forbids, that reading is void. This Article governs all the others.
