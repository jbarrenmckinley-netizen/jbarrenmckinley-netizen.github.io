A Record Kept in the Open

They were killed. The officer did not go to prison.
So their names are kept here — in the open, where no transfer, no file, and no quiet ruling can carry them out of it.

This is THE NOD: a verified record of cases in which an officer killed a civilian and did not go to prison — sorted by the form the outcome took, because the form is the story: the declined charge, the no-bill, the acquittal, the dropped case, the probation sentence. The fallen are named first, and with honor. The officers are named as the public record names them. Nothing here is rumor; every entry stands on the reported record.

And the record is kept in peace. The word over this page is the King’s: “Vengeance is the Lord’s — and so we forgive those who perpetrated it.” We remember so that it need not happen again — not to summon harm on anyone.

Remembrance Day — December 31

On December 31, all day, this Kingdom remembers the children and the men and women named here. Say their names.

The Anchor of This Record

Kohen Wiley · age 1

June 14, 2026 — Walmart parking lot, Senatobia, Mississippi

Sgt. Hunter Foster · Senatobia Police Department

Officers confronted two women over an alleged shoplifting of diapers and baby clothes — neither woman was ever charged. As the driver moved the car, Foster fired into it. Kohen, in his mother Vellesiya Wiley’s arms in the front passenger seat, was killed; the driver, Latoya Ferrell, was wounded. Kohen’s mother says she held the baby up to the window and warned the officers he was there.

Outcome — After a Mississippi Bureau of Investigation probe, Attorney General Lynn Fitch ruled the force justified on October 19, 2026, and did not take the case to a grand jury. Foster resigned days later — the interim chief also resigned — but he kept his state certification: legally hireable by any department in Mississippi.

No Criminal Charges Ever Filed

A prosecutor or attorney general reviewed the killing and declined. The officer was never charged with any crime.

Alton Sterling · age 37

July 5, 2016 — Baton Rouge, Louisiana

Officers Blane Salamoni and Howie Lake II · Baton Rouge Police Department

Officers responding to a report of a man with a gun outside a convenience store tackled Sterling to the ground. While he was pinned, one officer shouted that Sterling had a gun; Salamoni shot him multiple times at close range. Bystander and surveillance video spread nationwide.

Outcome — No charges. The U.S. Department of Justice declined federal charges in May 2017, and Louisiana Attorney General Jeff Landry declined state charges on March 27, 2018, calling the shooting “reasonable and justifiable.” Salamoni was fired March 30, 2018 (later converted to a resignation). The city settled the family’s civil suit for $4.5 million in 2021.

Stephon Clark · age 22

March 18, 2018 — Sacramento, California

Officers Terrence Mercadal and Jared Robinet · Sacramento Police Department

Pursuing a vandalism suspect, officers cornered Clark in his grandparents’ backyard and fired 20 rounds, later saying they believed the cellphone in his hand was a gun. Clark was unarmed; only a phone was found.

Outcome — No charges. Sacramento County DA Anne Marie Schubert declined to file charges on March 2, 2019; California AG Xavier Becerra’s independent review concurred on March 5, 2019; federal prosecutors also declined (September 26, 2019).

Keith Lamont Scott · age 43

September 20, 2016 — Charlotte, North Carolina

Officer Brentley Vinson · Charlotte-Mecklenburg Police Department

Plainclothes officers watching for an unrelated suspect encountered Scott in an apartment parking lot. Police said Scott held a handgun and ignored commands to drop it; his family said he was reading a book. Vinson shot him four times. Scott’s wife filmed the encounter’s aftermath on her phone.

Outcome — No charges. Mecklenburg County DA Andrew Murray declined to charge on November 30, 2016, ruling Vinson “acted lawfully,” and said a panel of 15 prosecutors was unanimous.

Tony Robinson · age 19

March 6, 2015 — Madison, Wisconsin

Officer Matthew “Matt” Kenny · Madison Police Department

Responding to calls about a man running in traffic and assaulting people, Kenny entered an apartment stairwell and shot the unarmed Robinson seven times. Kenny said Robinson knocked him down and kept coming; there were no independent witnesses to the shooting itself.

Outcome — No charges. Dane County DA Ismael Ozanne declined to charge on May 12, 2015, ruling a “lawful use of deadly force.” The city insurer settled the family’s civil suit for $3.35 million in 2017.

December 23, 2021 — North Hollywood, Los Angeles, California

Jones fired three rifle rounds at a man who had attacked shoppers with a bike lock and carried no gun. One round skipped off the floor, pierced a dressing-room wall, and killed Valentina, who was hiding and praying with her mother. The chief and the Police Commission found shots out of policy.

The Grand Jury Declined to Indict

The case went to a grand jury. The grand jury said no.

Michael Brown · age 18

August 9, 2014 — Ferguson, Missouri

Officer Darren Wilson · Ferguson Police Department

Wilson stopped Brown and a friend walking in the street; a struggle at the patrol car’s window was followed by Wilson firing 12 shots as Brown stood in the street, killing the unarmed teenager. Accounts of Brown’s final posture sharply conflicted. The shooting ignited the Ferguson protests.

Outcome — No indictment. A St. Louis County grand jury returned a “no true bill” on November 24, 2014. The U.S. Department of Justice later concluded Wilson acted in self-defense. Wilson resigned November 29, 2014.

Eric Garner · age 43

July 17, 2014 — Staten Island, New York

Officer Daniel Pantaleo · New York City Police Department

Officers stopping Garner on suspicion of selling untaxed cigarettes took him to the ground; Pantaleo held him around the neck in a chokehold as Garner repeated “I can’t breathe” eleven times on bystander video. The medical examiner ruled the death a homicide.

Outcome — No indictment. A Richmond County grand jury declined to indict on December 3, 2014. The DOJ declined federal civil-rights charges on July 16, 2019. Pantaleo was fired in August 2019 after a departmental trial. New York City settled with the family for $5.9 million.

Tamir Rice · age 12

November 22, 2014 — Cleveland, Ohio

Officers Timothy Loehmann and Frank Garmback · Cleveland Division of Police

A 911 caller reported someone pointing a gun at a recreation center, twice noting it was “probably fake” and the person “probably a juvenile” — information not relayed to the officers. The cruiser stopped feet from Tamir, and Loehmann shot him within about two seconds of arriving. The gun was an airsoft replica.

Outcome — No indictment. Cuyahoga County Prosecutor Timothy McGinty announced on December 28, 2015, that the grand jury had declined to indict either officer.

Daniel Prude · age 41

March 23, 2020 — Rochester, New York (died a week later)

Rochester Police Department — the three officers who held him down were Mark Vaughn, Francisco Santiago, and Troy Taladay

Prude, in a mental-health crisis, was handcuffed naked in the street, hooded with a “spit sock,” and held face-down by officers for about two minutes. He stopped breathing; the medical examiner ruled the death a homicide by asphyxia in physical restraint.

Outcome — No indictment. A grand jury voted not to indict any officer; Attorney General Letitia James announced the result on February 23, 2021.

Ma’Khia Bryant · age 16

April 20, 2021 — Columbus, Ohio

Officer Nicholas Reardon · Columbus Division of Police

Officers arrived at a foster home on a reported attempted stabbing. Body camera shows Bryant mid-swing with a knife toward another young woman when Reardon fired four shots, killing her.

Outcome — No indictment. A Franklin County grand jury declined to indict Reardon; announced March 11, 2022.

Acquitted at Trial

Charged, tried, and found not guilty — the nod in its courtroom form.

Sean Bell · age 23

November 25, 2006 — Jamaica, Queens, New York

NYPD detectives Michael Oliver (31 shots), Gescard Isnora (11), and Marc Cooper (4)

Undercover detectives who believed someone in Bell’s bachelor-party group had a gun fired 50 rounds into his car outside a club, on the morning of his wedding day. No weapon was ever recovered. Bell was killed; two friends were wounded.

Outcome — Acquitted. Oliver and Isnora were charged with manslaughter, Cooper with reckless endangerment. In a bench trial, Justice Arthur Cooperman acquitted all three on April 25, 2008. Federal prosecutors declined civil-rights charges. The city settled for $3.25 million.

Philando Castile · age 32

July 6, 2016 — Falcon Heights, Minnesota

Officer Jeronimo Yanez · St. Anthony Police Department

At a traffic stop, Castile calmly told Yanez he had a licensed firearm; seconds later Yanez fired seven shots into the car. Castile’s girlfriend, Diamond Reynolds, livestreamed the aftermath to Facebook as Castile died beside her, her young daughter in the back seat.

Outcome — Acquitted. Charged with second-degree manslaughter and two counts of dangerous discharge, Yanez was acquitted on June 16, 2017, and fired the same day. Settlements totaled roughly $3.8 million.

Terence Crutcher · age 40

September 16, 2016 — Tulsa, Oklahoma

Officer Betty Jo Shelby · Tulsa Police Department

Crutcher’s SUV stalled in the road. Helicopter and dashcam video show him walking with his hands up beside the vehicle; Shelby shot him once. He was unarmed; no weapon was found in the SUV.

Outcome — Acquitted. Charged with first-degree manslaughter within a week of the shooting, Shelby was acquitted on May 17, 2017. She was reinstated, resigned in August 2017, and became a Rogers County sheriff’s deputy.

Daniel Shaver · age 26

January 18, 2016 — Mesa, Arizona

Officer Philip “Mitch” Brailsford · Mesa Police Department

Responding to a report of a man with a gun at a hotel window — Shaver, a pest-control worker, had a pellet rifle for work — officers ordered the unarmed Shaver to crawl down a hallway under shouted, conflicting commands. On his knees, crying and begging not to be shot, he reached toward his waistband; Brailsford fired five rounds from his AR-15 and killed him.

Outcome — Acquitted. A Maricopa County jury acquitted Brailsford of second-degree murder and of the lesser charge of reckless manslaughter on December 7, 2017. He had been fired in March 2016; he later received a medical-retirement pension from the city.

Steven Clark · age 34

May 10, 2023 — Porthmadog, Gwynedd, North Wales, United Kingdom

PC Richard Williams · North Wales Police

Survived — kept in this record

Officers responding to a disturbance arrested Clark outside a home. Williams held him in a headlock on the grass and struck him nine times in the head — blows Williams called “distraction blows” — and incapacitant spray was used. A neighbour filmed the whole sequence. Clark survived, with swelling and bruising; at the misconduct hearing he testified, “I couldn’t breathe.” His is the first entry in this record from the United Kingdom — kept here by the King’s ruling, to show the reach of the pattern: the nod is not one country’s alone.

Outcome — Acquitted, then cleared. After an Independent Office for Police Conduct investigation, Williams was tried at Caernarfon Crown Court and acquitted in May 2025 of assault occasioning actual bodily harm and non-fatal strangulation. In September 2026 an independent misconduct panel found all eight counts of gross misconduct not proven and ruled the force reasonable. Deputy Chief Constable Nigel Harrison confirmed the matter is “now at an end”; Williams returned to full duties. No civil lawsuit has been reported.

Count the nods — how many hands it took to get him off: the judge who presided at Caernarfon Crown Court; the jury, on both counts — assault occasioning actual bodily harm, and non-fatal strangulation; the prosecutor — the Crown Prosecution Service, which let the acquittal stand as the end of the criminal road; the investigators — the Independent Office for Police Conduct, whose own process ended in a hearing that cleared him; the misconduct panel — all eight counts of gross misconduct, not proven; his boss, the chief — Deputy Chief Constable Nigel Harrison, who declared the matter “now at an end”; and his immediate command, who returned him to full duties. Seven doors. Every one nodded.

Convicted — But No Prison

The rarest form: a conviction on paper, and the officer still walked home.

Akai Gurley · age 28

November 20, 2014 — Brooklyn, New York

Officer Peter Liang · New York City Police Department

Rookie officer Liang, on a “vertical patrol” with his gun drawn in a darkened housing-project stairwell, fired once — he said he was startled. The bullet ricocheted off a wall and killed Gurley, an unarmed father who had just entered the stairwell a floor below. Neither Liang nor his partner rendered aid.

Outcome — Manslaughter conviction reduced; probation, no jail. A jury convicted Liang of second-degree manslaughter on February 11, 2016. On April 19, 2016, Judge Danny Chun reduced the conviction to criminally negligent homicide and sentenced Liang to five years’ probation and 800 hours of community service — no incarceration. The city settled with the family for over $4 million.

The Anti-Bank — When the System Answered

The other rail of the record, kept with the same standard: when the system did the right thing — immunity refused, certification revoked, restitution paid — it is duly noted here.

Founding Entry · AB1

K’aun Green · survived

March 27, 2022 — La Victoria Taqueria, San Jose, California

Officer Mark McNamara · San Jose Police Department

A brawl broke out in the taqueria; a man pulled a gun; Green — a Contra Costa College football player in town on a campus visit — wrestled the gun away from him. Backing out the door holding the weapon, Green met officers arriving on a homicide report. Surveillance video showed Green dropping the gun and raising his hands; McNamara fired five shots, striking Green four times. Part of Green’s intestine was removed. He was handcuffed to his hospital gurney until the footage cleared him; the actual gunman was arrested.

And this time, the system answered:

  • Qualified immunity denied — by the trial court, and again by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit (September 2025): a reasonable jury could find McNamara violated Green’s Fourth Amendment rights by shooting him as he surrendered.
  • The badge taken — McNamara’s racist text messages about Green surfaced through the department’s own chief; McNamara resigned in November 2023 and lost his California law-enforcement certification.
  • Restitution at record scale — the City of San Jose settled for $8 million, the largest police-shooting settlement in the city’s history.
  • The man restored — Green earned a full scholarship to the University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff and is on track to be the first college graduate in his family.

AB2

Terrence DeBose · survived

March 22, 2019 — Cuyahoga County Jail, Cleveland, Ohio

Corporal Nicholas Evans · Officer Timothy Dugan · Cuyahoga County Jail

DeBose, 47, a detainee with a lifelong mental illness, was strapped into a restraint chair — arms, shoulders, head, and legs — wearing a spit hood, unable to raise a hand to his own face. Evans reached up and switched off his body camera. The ceiling camera kept recording: Evans struck DeBose six times, Dugan twice more, DeBose’s head snapping against the chair’s restraints. The guards then left him restrained and alone for some two hours. He was diagnosed with post-concussion syndrome.

And this time, the system answered:

  • Guilty pleas — on October 15, 2019, Evans pleaded guilty to attempted felonious assault and to tampering with evidence: the switched-off camera was itself a felony. Dugan pleaded guilty to attempted abduction and misdemeanor assault.
  • Prison — on February 26, 2020, Judge John O’Donnell of the Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas sentenced Evans to nine months in prison, with three years of supervision after release. Both men resigned.
  • The light end, kept in the open — Dugan received ten days in jail. DeBose called the sentences “a slap on the wrist.” This record keeps both rails in view: the system answered, and exactly how far its answer reached.
  • Civil restitution — DeBose’s federal lawsuit against the county was settled through mediation in July 2024; the amount was not disclosed.
