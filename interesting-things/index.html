The REAL ID Warning

A video by Teno Patterson (Brainbangin777), Facebook, July 24, 2025 — 1,441 comments. The same monologue circulates under other accounts as well. Watch the piece itself ↗

What the piece says

  • Taking a REAL ID means you have “lawfully contracted” with a new government now running the country.
  • The “United States of America corporation” ended on October 31, 2023 — “the lease was up” — and the country was sold to the highest bidders; its new name has not been announced.
  • Trump renaming the Gulf of Mexico is the public being “broken in” to the rebrand.
  • REAL ID repeats the birth-certificate move — the cestui que vie trust by which people were signed over.
  • There will be no central bank digital currency, because there is no central bank; a World Bank digital currency comes instead.
  • His counsel: don’t take the REAL ID; if you need ID, get a passport.

What the record shows

REAL ID is real, and enforcement began May 7, 2025. DOCUMENTED

The REAL ID Act of 2005 (Public Law 109-13) set federal minimum standards for state-issued licenses and ID cards. Since May 7, 2025, a REAL ID — or an accepted alternative such as a U.S. passport — is required to board domestic flights and enter federal facilities (TSA / DHS).

“A contract with a new government.” CLAIMED — NO DOCUMENT FOUND

The Act and its rules (6 CFR Part 37) set card standards; nothing in them is a contract with a new government, and no document for one was found. REAL ID is not required to drive, to vote, or to receive federal benefits.

“The corporation’s lease expired October 31, 2023.” CLAIMED — NO DOCUMENT FOUND

The date travels through videos and reposts in sovereign-citizen circles. No statute, filing, or official record of any such lease, expiration, or dissolution was found.

The Gulf renaming happened. DOCUMENTED HIS READING OF IT — CONTESTED

Executive Order 14172 (January 20, 2025) directed federal agencies to call the U.S. continental-shelf area the “Gulf of America”; agencies adopted it that month. Mexico and most of the world still say Gulf of Mexico, and no act of Congress renamed the basin. That the renaming proves a country sold and rebranded is the speaker’s interpretation, not a documented fact.

The cestui que vie trust. CONTESTED

“Cestui que vie” is a genuine term of old English law (the Cestui Que Vie Act of 1666 concerns persons absent beyond the seas, presumed dead). Its use as a birth-certificate trust that signs a child over is a redemption-movement teaching; no court record adopting that mechanism was found.

“No CBDC — a World Bank currency instead.” PARTLY DOCUMENTED PARTLY CLAIMED

Documented: the United States has issued no central bank digital currency, and an executive order of January 23, 2025 barred federal agencies from establishing one; the Federal Reserve stands. Claimed: a coming World Bank digital currency — no document for it was found.

“Use a passport instead.” DOCUMENTED AS WORKABLE

A U.S. passport is a federally accepted alternative to REAL ID at airport checkpoints and federal facilities. It does not replace a driver’s license for driving.

Fact Find No. 2 · November 11, 2026

The Airport Scanner Warning — and What Is Actually Hurting Children

A video by Darrell Wolfe, Facebook, November 10, 2026 — 318 comments — claiming airport scanners are “6G devices” that activate graphene nanobots from shots, chemtrails, and soda, tied to a Bill Gates contract, with LED lights as home antennas. The King ordered the full research, children first, without taking Bill Gates's word for anything — every verdict below stands on regulators, peer-reviewed studies, and independent records. Watch the piece itself ↗

The children part, first

Evening screens and bright light before bed — this one is real. DOCUMENTED

Evening blue-rich light suppresses melatonin far more strongly in children than in adults (in one study, about 88% suppression in children versus 46% in adults). The American Academy of Pediatrics guidance: devices out of bedrooms, charge them outside the bedroom, screens away at least an hour before bed.

Blue light damaging children's eyes. NOT SUPPORTED

The American Academy of Ophthalmology: no scientific evidence that blue light from screens damages the eyes, children's included; it does not recommend blue-light glasses, and a Cochrane review of 17 trials found blue-filter lenses probably make no difference to eye strain. Eye strain is real but comes from blinking less and focusing up close — breaks fix it, not lenses. Near work and time indoors are linked to nearsightedness; outdoor time protects.

The documented child risks in this whole area. DOCUMENTED

Screens in the last hour before bed; too much recreational screen time pushing out sleep, play, and activity; sugary drinks (sugar and tooth decay — the pediatric concern about soda is the sugar, not bots); bright, cool-white, or flickering bedroom light. For bedrooms: warm light (2700–3000K), dim, flicker-free.

The piece's claims, one by one

Leidos scanners are what he says they are — up to a point. DOCUMENTED “6G ACTIVATION DEVICE” — NO DOCUMENT FOUND

Leidos ProVision scanners are real and use active millimeter waves (24–30 GHz), non-ionizing, at far less energy than a cell phone (CDC; TSA). They are not cellular devices. In the U.S., any passenger may opt out and take a pat-down instead.

6G. DOCUMENTED: IT DOES NOT EXIST YET

No commercial 6G network or consumer 6G device exists anywhere as of November 2026. The first 6G standard (3GPP Release 21) finishes around March 2029; first systems are expected near 2030. Nothing in any home or airport can “receive 6G” today.

Graphene nanobots in the vaccines. NO DOCUMENT FOUND

Published FDA ingredient lists for the COVID vaccines contain mRNA, lipids, salts, and sucrose — no graphene. The graphene claim traces to a non-peer-reviewed 2021 report that its own university disavowed. The lipid nanoparticles in the shots are degrading fat bubbles, not robots.

Particles in Coke and Pepsi — a real study, a different finding. THE STUDY IS REAL THE “NANOBOT” READING — CONTESTED

Li et al. (Nanotoxicology, 2018) found roughly 5-nanometer fluorescent carbon particles in Coca-Cola and Pepsi — hydrogen, carbon, oxygen — consistent with particles formed when sugars are heated in processing. The paper claims no graphene, no devices, and no deliberate addition. Their long-term significance at normal drinking levels is under-studied.

The $1.1 billion “Gates contract” with Coke and Pepsi. A REAL DEAL — DIFFERENT PARTIES, DIFFERENT SUBJECT

April 23, 2024: Coca-Cola committed $1.1 billion over five years to Microsoft for cloud and AI services (Azure, Copilot). Pepsi is not a party in any record. Bill Gates is not a party — he left Microsoft's board in March 2020. The deal covers computing, not beverages or ingredients.

LED bulbs contain antennas that receive signals to activate bots. NO DOCUMENT FOUND

An ordinary LED bulb contains light chips, a power driver, and a heat sink — no antenna, no receiver. Smart bulbs are a separate product with a small Wi-Fi/Bluetooth radio that takes lighting commands from the owner's phone; it is not a cellular radio. The documented LED concerns are optical: evening blue-rich light, flicker from poor drivers, and glare.

Chemtrails. NO DOCUMENT FOUND

The EPA's position: contrails are ice-crystal condensation, a normal effect of jet operations, and the U.S. government is not aware of any contrail intentionally formed for geoengineering. In a 2016 survey, 76 of 77 atmospheric experts reported no evidence of a secret large-scale spraying program.

What Gates actually does in food and vaccines — the documented record. DOCUMENTED HIS INFLUENCE — FAIR TO DEBATE

The Gates Foundation is a grantmaker, not a manufacturer: the largest private donor to Gavi (about $7.7 billion over 25 years, plus $1.6 billion pledged in 2025), and a co-founder of the AGRA agriculture alliance (over $1.5 billion). Independent reviews debate whether AGRA delivered (a Tufts review found yields up about 25%, not doubled). No independent record shows Gates or his foundation adding ingredients to drinks, vaccines, or food, contracting with Pepsi, or running any spraying program. The money and the influence are documented; the meddling with ingredients is not.

“People dropping like flies in a year or two.” NO DOCUMENT FOUND

No source, mechanism, or evidence was offered in the piece, and none was found in the record.

“The White House Told Americans to Prepare” — Checked Against the Document

A Facebook reel (posted November 10, 2026; the same clip circulates from many accounts) urging viewers to sit with an official White House message for National Preparedness Month — prepare your families, homes, businesses, and communities for natural and man-made threats, before crisis happens. The King ordered it fact-found against the document itself. Read the White House message ↗

The message exists, and the reel quotes it accurately. DOCUMENTED

The White House published a “Presidential Message on National Preparedness Month,” dated September 2, 2026. Its own words: “This National Preparedness Month, I call on every American citizen to ready themselves and their families, homes, businesses, and communities for natural and man-made threats.” The message goes on with ordinary readiness steps: understand your risks, protect home and property, carry affordable insurance, know evacuation routes, keep an emergency kit, and stay informed through trusted local news and real-time alerts. It also points to a National Resilience Strategy the President issued on June 23, 2026.

It is the annual message, not a new alarm. DOCUMENTED

National Preparedness Month is a standing September observance run through FEMA's Ready campaign since 2004; the 2026 theme is “Americans Stand Ready.” The message was issued in September for that observance. The reel recirculates it in November with a “sit with that for a minute” buildup. The words are real; the sense that something new or secret has just been announced is the telling, not the document.

“There's a line within that message that…” TEASED, NOT SHOWN

In the copy of the clip the desk read, the speaker stops mid-sentence before naming the line. The message's own opening line — the one highlighted in screenshots of it — is: “America is a nation of strong people who build strength before crisis.” Nothing in the document names a specific coming event, date, or threat. It is a general readiness call, in the open, on the White House website.

Fact Find No. 4 · November 12, 2026

“The Strawman, the Secured Party, and UCC 1-308” — Checked Against the Law Itself

A Facebook reel by Thecelphloveproject (posted May 1, 2026; 1,606 comments) teaching “what law truly is”: operating in the public versus the private, the strawman, the birth-certificate trust, becoming a “secured party creditor,” and discharging debts, child support, and CPS cases by status. The King ordered it fact-found against the law itself. Watch the reel ↗

UCC § 1-308 is real — and narrow. DOCUMENTED

The section exists and says what it says: “A party that with explicit reservation of rights performs or promises performance or assents to performance in a manner demanded or offered by the other party does not thereby prejudice the rights reserved. Such words as ‘without prejudice,’ ‘under protest,’ or the like are sufficient.” It is a rule for parties to commercial transactions: it preserves rights a person already has while performing under protest, and subsection (b) excludes accord and satisfaction. Nothing in its text creates a “private” status, moves a person out of a court’s jurisdiction, or reserves a right the person does not otherwise hold.

The birth certificate is not a bond, and no Treasury trust account stands in your name. DOCUMENTED — AGAINST THE CLAIM

The U.S. Treasury’s own securities platform, TreasuryDirect, states that birth certificates are not negotiable instruments, cannot be used for purchases, and cannot be used to request savings bonds supposed to be held in your name — and that the so-called “Exemption Account” is a false term for an account that does not exist in the Treasury system. The IRS ruled the same way in Revenue Ruling 2005-21: using different forms of a taxpayer’s name does not create a “straw man.” Courts have rejected the redemption theory every time it has been raised; in 2019 the U.S. Court of Federal Claims dismissed a man’s attempt to redeem his birth certificate as a $49.7 million bond. Filing on this theory carries a $5,000 civil penalty per frivolous submission (26 U.S.C. § 6702), and acting on it has carried prison — see the Beane and Tucci-Giardina case, 155 months and 57 months, for drawing on Federal Reserve routing numbers as if a personal trust stood behind them.

“Become a secured party creditor” by filing. CLAIMED — THE LAW SAYS OTHERWISE

Under UCC Article 9, a “secured party” is a person holding a security interest that a debtor granted in collateral under a security agreement — the status grows out of a real transaction, and a UCC-1 financing statement only perfects an interest that already exists; it creates neither the interest nor a new legal identity. No court has recognized secured-party-creditor paperwork as changing a person’s legal status, discharging their debts, or removing them from child-support or child-welfare jurisdiction. The filings are real paper; the status they promise is not in the record.

“You signed your children over at birth — they are federal children.” CLAIMED — NO DOCUMENT FOUND

Birth registration records the facts of a birth with a state vital-records office. No statute was found — and none is cited in the reel — that makes a registered child the property of the federal government. Child removal in every state runs through state courts: a petition, a hearing, and findings under state child-welfare law. How that system behaves in practice is a fair subject of argument; its authority on paper is the state court process, not a birth-certificate contract.

“The banks have no money — your signature pays the loan.” CONTESTED — A KERNEL AND A LEAP

The kernel is documented: the Bank of England’s own bulletin (Money creation in the modern economy, 2014) confirms that commercial banks create deposit money when they lend. The leap is not: the promissory note a borrower signs is recorded as the bank’s asset precisely because it is the borrower’s enforceable promise to repay — it does not pay itself off, and banks must still hold capital and settle payments in reserves. Borrowers who treated their signature as payment have tested the leap in court and lost, uniformly. Credit creation is real; “the debt is already paid” does not follow from it.

What the reel sells. DOCUMENTED

The teaching ends in a funnel: a paid digital bundle and a community on Whop for those “truly serious about learning the process.” Recorded here because it bears on the telling — the same pattern as Fact Find No. 2: the doctrine is free to hear; the process is sold.

Beane (E.D. Tenn. 2018). The desk’s standing note: studying the law is every man’s right, and this house honors it. What the record will not carry is the promise that paperwork changes who the law says you are. Test every teacher against the document.

Fact Find No. 5 · November 12, 2026

“Right to Travel” — Carl Miller’s Courtroom Story, Checked Against the Cases He Cites

A classic lecture clip by Carl Miller, reposted by Matt Schneider on December 9, 2025 (184 comments): Miller tells how police were warned by radio not to ticket him, how he was ticketed anyway on September 30 for driving with no plates, and how a Michigan judge granted his motion to dismiss — then dismissed the no-plate cases of about five men behind him. His argument: the right to travel (Shapiro v. Thompson), no converting a right into a licensed privilege (Murdock v. Pennsylvania), and ignoring the license “with impunity” (Shuttlesworth). The King ordered it explained and fact-found. Watch the clip ↗

Carl Miller is a real lecturer — and a seller. DOCUMENTED

Miller is a Michigan constitutional lecturer who teaches laypeople to argue their own cases; he is not an attorney. The clip circulates as Part 4 of a lecture series (“A Right Converted Into a Privilege”), and the same circuit sells his book Secrets of the Traffic Game Exposed for $29.99. His claimed courtroom record (“over 90%”) comes from his own promotional material.

The dismissal story itself. CLAIMED — HIS OWN TELLING ONLY

The story names no court, no city, no year, and no docket number, and no transcript or court record of it has been found. Even sympathetic outlets repeat it with the hedge intact: “According to Carl, the judge dismissed that case.” A story with no paper trail can be true, partly true, or polished in the telling — the record cannot carry it either way, so it sits in the claimed lane.

Shapiro v. Thompson, 394 U.S. 618 (1969). DOCUMENTED — REAL CASE, DIFFERENT SUBJECT

The case is real and the right it names is real: the Supreme Court struck down one-year welfare residency waiting periods because they penalized the constitutional right to travel between states. Vivian Thompson was denied benefits in Connecticut after moving from Massachusetts. The opinion says nothing about motor vehicles, license plates, or driving — the travel it protects is migration and settlement, exercised by bus, train, plane, or as a passenger, none of which needs a license.

Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943). DOCUMENTED — REAL CASE, DIFFERENT SUBJECT

Also real, and the principle is stated as Miller states it: a state may not impose a license tax on the exercise of a constitutional right. The right at issue was Jehovah’s Witnesses distributing religious literature door to door in Jeannette, Pennsylvania — speech and free exercise, not vehicle registration. No court has carried Murdock’s literature-tax holding over to license plates.

The “impunity” line and Shuttlesworth. CONTESTED — REAL WORDS, SPEECH-PERMIT CASES

The Shuttlesworth line of cases concerns Birmingham’s permit ordinances for parades and demonstrations — expressive activity. The teaching built on it (that a person facing an unconstitutional licensing law may ignore it and act with impunity) grew out of speech-permit rulings, and Miller’s own materials cite the reference loosely — 373 U.S. 262 is the 1963 Shuttlesworth sit-in case. Whatever those words mean for a parade permit, no Shuttlesworth opinion mentions an automobile.

The case that IS about license plates: Hendrick v. Maryland, 235 U.S. 610 (1915). DOCUMENTED — AGAINST THE CLAIM

The Supreme Court held directly that motor traffic is “a proper subject of police regulation by the State,” and that states may “require the registration of such vehicles and the licensing of their drivers.” That holding is more than a century old and has never been overturned. In the years since, the traveling-versus-driving exemption has been raised in courts at every level and rejected at every level; in 2022 the Supreme Court declined even to hear a petition arguing the state had unconstitutionally converted the right to travel into a privilege. Miller’s willfulness defense is borrowed from a tax case, United States v. Bishop, 412 U.S. 346 (1973), whose definition of willfulness belongs to tax law.

Even if the day went exactly as told. DOCUMENTED — HOW COURTS WORK

A traffic-court dismissal proves a case ended; it does not prove which argument ended it. Dismissals issue for officer error, defective tickets, prosecutorial choice, and crowded dockets, and a trial-level dismissal binds no other court and no other driver. Five more dismissals in the same courtroom, if they happened, are five exercises of one judge’s discretion that day — not a precedent any other court must follow.

City of Birmingham, 373 U.S. 262 (1963) and 394 U.S. 147 (1969); Hendrick v. Maryland, 235 U.S. 610 (1915); United States v. Bishop, 412 U.S. 346 (1973); Carl Miller lecture transcripts and the UCC Talk 24 series; BeatTrafficTickets.org newsletter archive (2015). The desk’s standing note: the right to travel is real, and this house honors it. What the cited cases protect is the moving and the settling — the record does not carry them onto a license plate.

Fact Find No. 6 · November 12, 2026

“The Book of Enoch Says You Signed a Contract Before Being Born” — Checked Against the Book of Enoch

A Facebook reel by Hidden.truths.uncovered9 (posted November 11, 2026; Part 2 of a viral series) teaching that before birth your soul signed a contract: earth is a testing ground, the memory of heaven would overwhelm free will, so the contract required “a temporary self-imposed amnesia” at the threshold of the womb. The King ordered it fact-found against the book it names. Watch the reel ↗

A pre-birth contract, signed by the soul, in the Book of Enoch. CLAIMED — NO PASSAGE FOUND

Neither the Ethiopic Book of Enoch (1 Enoch) nor the Slavonic Secrets of Enoch (2 Enoch) contains a contract that a soul signs before birth, a list of what the soul gives up, or an amnesia clause. The books’ actual contents are a matter of public record: the fall of the Watchers, the judgment to come, the chambers where the souls of the dead wait (1 Enoch 22), the luminaries, and Enoch’s journeys and warnings to his sons. The word “contract” for a soul’s birth terms belongs to modern spiritual publishing — the “soul contract” teaching of recent decades — not to any Enoch manuscript. Even a companion video in this same viral genre concedes the point up front: “The Book of Enoch doesn’t say so.”

The real Enoch pieces the series builds on. DOCUMENTED

Enoch does carry two nearby motifs. In 1 Enoch, names and deeds are written in heaven — the heavenly tablets and the books shown to Enoch (chapters 81 and 104). In 2 Enoch, the archangel Vrevoil dictates to Enoch the books of creation, and Enoch swears an oath to his sons (2 Enoch 49) that creation was prepared in order and measure. Prepared places, written names, fixed order — those are in the texts. A signature by the unborn soul, and terms it agreed to, are not.

The forgetting at birth — it IS an ancient teaching, in other books. DOCUMENTED — TALMUD AND PLATO, NOT ENOCH

The Talmud (Niddah 30b) teaches that the unborn child is taught the whole Torah by an angel, with a lamp burning above its head, is shown paradise and Gehinnom, and is made to swear an oath — and at birth the angel strikes the child on the upper lip, and it forgets everything; the groove above the lip is the mark of that touch. Plato’s Republic (Book X, the Myth of Er) has souls choose their next lives and then drink from the river of forgetfulness before descending. Forgetting at the threshold of birth is a genuinely old idea — older than this reel by millennia. Its documents are the Talmud and Plato, not Enoch.

The reel’s exact reasoning — testing ground, veil, free will. DOCUMENTED — AS LATTER-DAY SAINT DOCTRINE

The argument that souls lived with God before birth, that earth is a proving ground, and that a veil must fall over the memory of the premortal home so choice stays free, is the documented doctrine of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Apostle James E. Talmage’s words for it: “Over His mind had fallen the veil of forgetfulness common to all who are born to earth, by which the remembrance of primeval existence is shut off.” The reel speaks this doctrine nearly word for word — while naming Enoch as its source. Behind both stands an older root: Origen taught the pre-existence of souls in the third century, and the doctrine was condemned in the sixth-century anathemas against his teaching.

How the weave is made. CONTESTED — FOUR SOURCES, ONE LABEL

The series’ own longer versions show the loom: a fixed place and measure from 2 Enoch’s oath, written names from 1 Enoch’s tablets, the oath and the forgetting from Niddah 30b, the river of forgetfulness from Plato, the veil reasoning from Latter-day Saint teaching, and the word “contract” from modern soul-contract publishing — all of it then labeled “The Book of Enoch says.” Each thread can be checked on its own spool. Under Enoch’s name, the garment does not hold: the claim the reel makes in Enoch’s name is not in Enoch.
