THE DOSSIER — SILENCED VOICES

Disappearances, Suspicious Deaths, and Those Who Died After Speaking

HOW TO READ THIS DOSSIER

Every entry in this dossier keeps three lines separate, and the reader should hold us to it:

  1. WHAT IS DOCUMENTED — facts carried by court records, official reports, contemporaneous reporting, or the person's own published words, with sources and dates.
  2. WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS — the ruling, finding, or statement of the authority that handled the case.
  3. WHAT IS ASSERTED — claims made by families, supporters, named critics, or investigators, attributed to whoever made them. An assertion is printed because it is part of the record of the case — not because this page has proven it.

This dossier does not state that any death was a killing unless a court or an official record establishes it. Where the record is thin, the entry says so. The strength of this page is that it does not need to exaggerate: the documented floor is dark enough.

They had something to say contrary to the elite and the status quo, and they were systematically targeted for saying it: the scientists, the inventors, the witnesses, the ones whose work could topple markets or expose projects that were never meant to be known. These are not coincidences. See them for what they are. The entries below give you the record — documented, official, and asserted, line by line — so you can weigh it with your own heart.

SECTION A — DISAPPEARED

A1. Raoul Wallenberg (August 4, 1912 – disappeared January 17, 1945)

Who he was / what he did: Swedish diplomat posted to Budapest in 1944, where he issued protective passports and maintained safe houses that saved thousands of Hungarian Jews from deportation and murder in the final months of the Second World War — in defiance of the Nazi regime and its Hungarian collaborators, and with backing from the U.S. War Refugee Board.

What happened: On January 17, 1945, during the Siege of Budapest, Soviet SMERSH officers detained Wallenberg on suspicion of espionage and took him toward Marshal Malinovsky's headquarters. His last recorded words were: "I'm going to Malinovsky's… whether as a guest or prisoner I do not know yet." He was never seen in public again.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The detention itself, and that the Soviet Union gave shifting, contradictory accounts for decades: Soviet-controlled radio announced in March 1945 that Wallenberg had been murdered in Budapest; Deputy Foreign Minister Vyshinsky stated in August 1947 that Wallenberg was not in the Soviet Union — a statement Soviet authorities later admitted was untrue (Hansard, UK Parliament, July 23, 1982; University of Michigan Wallenberg Legacy).
  • In 1957 the USSR produced a handwritten prison document dated July 17, 1947, reporting that "the prisoner Wallenberg… died last night in his cell" (Gromyko memorandum, 1957; sweden.se).
  • In 1991, Vyacheslav Nikonov, assigned by the Russian government to investigate, concluded Wallenberg had likely been executed by Soviet authorities in late 1947; a document reflecting that inquiry was released in 2023 as part of the JFK Assassination Records Collection. In 2000, Russian official Alexander Yakovlev announced Wallenberg had been executed in Lubyanka prison in 1947, and Russia posthumously rehabilitated Wallenberg and his driver as victims of political repression (Wikipedia, summarizing the Swedish-Russian working group record; sweden.se).
  • Sweden declared Wallenberg dead in absentia in October 2016, with a purely formal date of death of July 31, 1952 (Swedish Tax Agency, 2016).

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: The Soviet account of record (1957) is that Wallenberg died of a suspected heart attack (myocardial infarction) in a Lubyanka cell on July 17, 1947. Russia's later official posture (2000–2001) accepts he died in custody in 1947 but the joint Swedish-Russian working group concluded there is "no fully reliable proof" of the exact circumstances.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Fellow prisoners and later witnesses reported encountering a man matching Wallenberg in Soviet prisons and psychiatric hospitals into the 1980s — asserted by witnesses and maintained by his family for decades. Former Soviet spymaster Pavel Sudoplatov claimed in his memoir that Wallenberg was poisoned by Grigory Mairanovsky of the NKVD toxicology laboratory. Neither line has been proven.

Sources: Hansard (UK Parliament), July 23, 1982 · sweden.se, "Raoul Wallenberg" (accessed 2026) · University of Michigan, Wallenberg Legacy, "Disappearance and Unknown Fate" · Wikipedia, "Raoul Wallenberg" (summary of the Swedish-Russian working group and 2023 document release).

A2. Mehdi Ben Barka (1920 – disappeared October 29, 1965)

Who he was / what he said: Moroccan nationalist and socialist, head of the left-wing National Union of Popular Forces (UNFP) and secretary of the Tricontinental Conference — the leading opponent of King Hassan II and a prominent anti-imperialist voice. He had been sentenced to death in absentia in Morocco and was living in exile.

What happened: On October 29, 1965, outside the Brasserie Lipp in Paris, two French police officers stopped Ben Barka and put him into a car. He was never seen again. His body has never been found.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • A French trial in 1967 convicted participants in the abduction: intelligence fixer Antoine Lopez received eight years and police officer Louis Souchon six years for the illegal arrest; the court named Moroccan Interior Minister Mohamed Oufkir the principal responsible party, and France issued an international warrant for Oufkir, which Morocco ignored (French trial record, June 1967; Larousse judicial archive).
  • French police officers, criminal intermediaries, and persons linked to Moroccan state structures all had roles in the operation (French judicial investigation record).
  • The French judicial investigation remains open: in July–August 2025 the French Justice Ministry publicly confirmed the case is an ongoing judicial matter, and Ben Barka's son Bachir was again heard as a civil party (French National Assembly, Justice Ministry response, August 26, 2025).
  • In 1976 the U.S. government acknowledged, in response to FOIA requests, that the CIA held some 1,800 documents concerning Ben Barka; they were not released.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: There is no official account of Ben Barka's death from Morocco, and no final judicial account of the killing from France. President de Gaulle formally declared his government had not ordered the operation. The adjudicated facts end at the abduction.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Former Moroccan secret-police officer Ahmed Boukhari stated in 2001 that Ben Barka died under interrogation by Moroccan agents in a villa south of Paris and that his body was flown to Morocco and dissolved in acid (reported in Le Monde, June 2001; Human Rights Watch World Report 2002). Journalist Ronen Bergman concluded in Rise and Kill First (2018), from interviews with Israeli operatives, that Mossad located Ben Barka for Moroccan intelligence and disposed of his body after Moroccan agents and French police killed him. Time magazine reported in 1975 that three Moroccan agents, Oufkir among them, were responsible for the death. These accounts conflict in detail and none has been adjudicated.

Sources: French trial verdicts, June 1967 · Human Rights Watch, World Report 2002 (Morocco chapter) · French National Assembly, Justice Ministry response, August 26, 2025 · Time, "The Murder of Mehdi Ben Barka," December 29, 1975 · Ronen Bergman, Rise and Kill First (2018).

A3. Jimmy Hoffa (February 14, 1913 – disappeared July 30, 1975)

Who he was / what he carried: President of the International Brotherhood of Teamsters (1957–1971), the most powerful labor leader in America, convicted in 1964 of jury tampering and fraud, imprisoned 1967–1971, and released under a Nixon commutation barring him from union activity until 1980. In 1975 he was litigating to overturn that bar and retake the union — and he knew, in detail, the Teamsters' dealings with organized crime and the pension-fund money that ran through them.

What happened: On July 30, 1975, Hoffa drove to the Machus Red Fox restaurant in Bloomfield Township, Michigan, reportedly to meet Detroit mob figure Anthony Giacalone and New Jersey Teamsters official Anthony Provenzano. He was last seen in the parking lot between about 2:45 and 2:50 p.m. His car was found there. He was never seen again, and no body has ever been recovered.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • Both Giacalone and Provenzano denied the meeting took place; neither was ever charged (FBI HOFFEX investigation record; Britannica).
  • In 2001, DNA testing matched a strand of Hoffa's hair to the interior of a car belonging to Teamster Charles O'Brien, who had long denied Hoffa was ever in it (FBI; EBSCO Research Starters).
  • Hoffa was declared legally dead in absentia on July 30, 1982, by the Oakland County Probate Court on his family's petition. No charge for his killing has ever been filed against anyone; the FBI case (HOFFEX, opened August 1975) has never produced a prosecutable account of who took him, where he died, or what was done with his body.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: The FBI's stated position is that Hoffa was killed on or shortly after July 30, 1975, by individuals connected to the Provenzano faction of organized crime, acting with the knowledge of the Detroit family — i.e., an organized-crime homicide to keep him from returning to power. The operational details have never been established to a prosecutable standard.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Hitman Frank Sheeran confessed to shooting Hoffa, in Charles Brandt's I Heard You Paint Houses (2004); the confession has never been substantiated and is disputed by other investigators and journalists. Dozens of burial claims — under Giants Stadium, in landfills, in swamps, in a steel drum — have been asserted over fifty years and several publicized excavations; none has produced remains.

Sources: FBI HOFFEX file summaries and the 1976 "Hoffex Memo" (released 2006) · Oakland County Probate Court, order declaring James R. Hoffa legally dead, July 30, 1982 · Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Jimmy Hoffa" (updated Oct 7, 2026) · Detroit Historical Society, Encyclopedia of Detroit.

SECTION B — SUSPICIOUS DEATHS

B1. Karen Silkwood (February 19, 1946 – November 13, 1974)

Who she was / what she was carrying: Laboratory technician at Kerr-McGee's Cimarron plutonium fuel-rod plant near Crescent, Oklahoma, and the first woman on her local union's bargaining committee (Oil, Chemical and Atomic Workers). She had documented safety failures at the plant — spills, leaks, falsified inspection records, defective fuel rods — and had testified about them to the Atomic Energy Commission.

What happened: In the first week of November 1974 Silkwood was repeatedly contaminated with plutonium — at the plant on November 5, again November 6, and on November 7 her Edmond apartment was found contaminated, including food packaging in her refrigerator. On the evening of November 13, 1974, she left a union meeting at the Hub Cafe in Crescent carrying a notebook and folder of evidence, driving to Oklahoma City to deliver them to New York Times reporter David Burnham and union official Steve Wodka. Her white Honda Civic ran off Highway 74 into a culvert about seven miles south of Crescent. She was dead at the scene. The folder of documents was never recovered.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The contamination events of November 5–7, 1974, including the contamination of her apartment, are established in AEC and Kerr-McGee records and summarized by the U.S. Supreme Court in Silkwood v. Kerr-McGee Corp., 464 U.S. 238 (1984). Kerr-McGee suggested she had contaminated herself to embarrass the company; that claim was never proven either.
  • A union-retained accident investigator, A.O. Pipkin, found damage to the rear of her car — dents with metal and rubber fragments — that he concluded indicated impact from another vehicle (reported in the AltWeeklies AAN investigation and in Howard Kohn's Rolling Stone reporting, 1975–77).
  • In 1979 a federal jury found Kerr-McGee negligent in her plutonium contamination and awarded $10.5 million; after appeals, the family settled in 1986 for $1.38 million, with Kerr-McGee admitting no liability (Texas State Historical Association, Handbook of Texas; EBSCO).
  • The documents she carried that night were not in the car when union official Drew Stephens reached the scene hours later, and have never surfaced.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: The Oklahoma Highway Patrol ruled the crash an accident: Silkwood fell asleep at the wheel, with methaqualone (Quaalude) tablets found in her car and the drug present in her system. A federal (Justice Department) investigation likewise concluded the death was accidental and found no evidence of foul play.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Her family, union colleagues, and investigators including attorney Daniel Sheehan have asserted she was forced off the road — and some assert she was deliberately contaminated — to stop the documents from reaching the press. Those assertions rest on the bumper findings, the missing folder, and the contamination timeline; no court has adopted them as findings about her death.

Sources: Silkwood v. Kerr-McGee Corp., 464 U.S. 238 (1984) · Texas State Historical Association, "Silkwood, Karen Gay" · EBSCO Research Starters, "Silkwood Becomes a Symbol for the Antinuclear Movement" · Howard Kohn, Who Killed Karen Silkwood? (1981) · Richard Rashke, The Killing of Karen Silkwood (1981).

B2. Danny Casolaro (June 16, 1947 – August 10, 1991)

Who he was / what he was investigating: Freelance journalist investigating the INSLAW affair — the Justice Department's alleged theft of the PROMIS software — and what he believed was a connected network of intelligence, arms, and finance scandals linking INSLAW, Iran-Contra, the October Surprise, and BCCI. He called the network "the Octopus" and told his family he was close to finishing the story.

What happened: Casolaro traveled to Martinsburg, West Virginia, telling his brother he was going to meet a source and "bring back the head of the Octopus." On August 10, 1991, he was found dead in the bathtub of Room 517 of the Sheraton Inn, Martinsburg, with ten to twelve deep cuts to both wrists and a handwritten note nearby.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The Berkeley County medical examiner ruled the death a suicide by exsanguination. A second autopsy in January 1992 reaffirmed suicide (Washington Post, January 1992).
  • The handling of the scene was irregular on the record: the body was embalmed by a local funeral home before the family was notified and before an independent autopsy could be requested, and the bathwater was not retained — destroying toxicological and forensic evidence (family statements; multiple contemporaneous accounts).
  • The underlying scandal he was investigating was real in part: the House Judiciary Committee's report, The INSLAW Affair (H. Rept. 102-857, September 10, 1992), documented serious Justice Department misconduct in the INSLAW matter. That report concerns INSLAW; it is not a finding about Casolaro's death.
  • The Justice Department's own 1994 review of the death concluded: "There is no credible evidence that Mr. Casolaro was murdered" (DOJ review, September 27, 1994).

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Suicide. The Martinsburg ruling stood through the second autopsy and the DOJ's 1994 review, which reaffirmed it and rejected the homicide claims.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: His family asserts he was murdered: they state he had been receiving threatening late-night calls, that he told his brother if anything happened to him in Martinsburg it would not be an accident, and that his documented squeamishness about blood and medical tests makes a dozen self-inflicted wrist cuts implausible to them. A number of law-enforcement officials argued publicly that the death deserved further scrutiny. ABC News and Time, given his notes by the family, investigated and found no evidence of murder.

Sources: House Judiciary Committee, The INSLAW Affair, H. Rept. 102-857 (Sept 10, 1992) · U.S. Department of Justice, INSLAW review (Sept 27, 1994) · Washington Post, "Death of reporter ruled a suicide" (January 1992) · Vanity Fair, "Behold, A Pale Horse" (December 1991) · Wikipedia, "Danny Casolaro" (summary of the autopsy and review record).

B3. Dr. Frank Olson (July 17, 1910 – November 28, 1953)

Who he was / what he knew: Bacteriologist with the Army's biological-warfare program at Camp Detrick, Maryland, working alongside the CIA's Technical Services Staff — a man inside the most secret weapons work in America, who in his final weeks, by his family's account and colleagues' reports, had grown distressed about what he had seen, including interrogation and biological-weapons programs overseas.

What happened: On November 19, 1953, at a working retreat at Deep Creek Lake, Maryland, CIA officer Sidney Gottlieb spiked the after-dinner Cointreau with LSD. Olson was dosed without his knowledge. He became agitated and paranoid over the following days. In the early hours of November 28, 1953, he went through the window of Room 1018A of the Hotel Statler in New York — a room he was sharing with CIA officer Robert Lashbrook — and died on the sidewalk below. The CIA told his family he had suffered a breakdown and jumped. The family was not told about the LSD for twenty-two years.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The covert dosing is admitted fact: it surfaced in the Rockefeller Commission's 1975 report on CIA activities, and the CIA confirmed Olson had been given LSD nine days before his death (Rockefeller Commission, 1975; Church Committee record).
  • President Gerald Ford personally apologized to the Olson family at the White House, CIA Director William Colby apologized as well, and Congress passed a private bill awarding the family a $750,000 settlement in 1976 (contemporaneous reporting; family statements).
  • In 1994 the family exhumed the body. A forensic team led by George Washington University professor James Starrs found a large hematoma above Olson's left eye and chest injuries that the majority of the team concluded were sustained before the fall, not during it (Starrs forensic report, 1994; subsequent reporting).
  • The Manhattan District Attorney's office reopened the case as a homicide investigation in 1996; it brought no charges, and the manner of death has never been officially restored to a clean "suicide" on the strength of the original account. The sons' 2012 civil suit for withheld documents was dismissed in July 2013 in light of the 1976 settlement.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: The original 1953 ruling was suicide. The government's documented position since 1975 is narrower and stranger than a defense of that ruling: it admits the Agency dosed him without consent and concealed it for two decades, paid his family, and apologized — while never officially reclassifying the death or prosecuting anyone for it.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: His son Eric Olson has argued for decades that his father was murdered because he had become a security risk who might talk about the biological-weapons work. That is the family's position, supported in their view by the 1994 forensic findings; no court has adopted it.

Sources: Rockefeller Commission Report (1975) · Church Committee Final Report (1976) · James Starrs forensic examination (1994), as reported by the New York Times and others · U.S. District Court, D.D.C., Olson family suit (filed Nov 28, 2012; dismissed July 2013) · Wikipedia, "Frank Olson" (summary of the disclosure and exhumation record).

B4. Gary Webb (August 31, 1955 – December 10, 2004)

Who he was / what he wrote: Pulitzer Prize-winning investigative journalist for the San Jose Mercury News. In August 1996 his "Dark Alliance" series traced the crack-cocaine trade in Los Angeles to Nicaraguan traffickers tied to the CIA-backed Contras, and charged that the U.S. government had looked the other way. The series detonated nationally; under pressure from major newspapers and the intelligence community, the Mercury News retreated from the story in 1997, and Webb's mainstream career ended.

What happened: On December 10, 2004, Webb was found dead in his home in Carmichael, California, from two gunshot wounds to the head from a .38 revolver. He was 49, divorced, out of daily journalism, and about to lose the house. Suicide notes were found.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The two wounds are documented in the coroner's findings as described by the Los Angeles Times: the first shot passed through his face and exited his left cheek; the coroner's staff concluded the second shot struck an artery.
  • Sacramento County Coroner Robert Lyons took the unusual step of issuing a public statement because his office was flooded with calls; he said on the record: "It's unusual in a suicide case to have two shots, but it has been done in the past, and it is in fact a distinct possibility" (Los Angeles Times / Sacramento reporting, December 2004).
  • Webb's family accepts the suicide ruling (as reported by the Los Angeles Times and summarized in reference accounts). The dispute over his death comes from readers and commentators, not from his family.
  • On the underlying story, official reviews cut both ways and both are on the record: the DOJ Inspector General (December 1997) and the CIA Inspector General's reports (Frederick Hitz, 1998) confirmed that the CIA had relationships with and knowledge of Contra-linked drug traffickers and failed to act on or report it — while Hitz found no evidence that the CIA as an agency conspired to import cocaine into the United States.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Suicide, ruled by the Sacramento County Coroner's Office, December 2004.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Commentators and readers have asserted for twenty years that a man does not shoot himself twice in the head and that Webb was killed in retribution for "Dark Alliance." That assertion is widespread; it is not supported by a competing forensic finding, and it is not his family's position.

Sources: Los Angeles Times, reporting on the coroner's findings (December 2004) · Sacramento County Coroner statement (December 2004) · CIA Inspector General (Hitz) Report, Vols. I–II (1998) · DOJ Inspector General, The CIA-Contra-Crack Cocaine Controversy (December 1997) · Nick Schou, Kill the Messenger (2006).

B5. Sen. Paul Wellstone (July 21, 1944 – October 25, 2002)

Who he was / what he said: United States Senator from Minnesota, the Senate's most outspoken liberal — and, in the autumn of 2002, its most prominent opponent of the rush to war in Iraq. On October 11, 2002, he voted against the Iraq war authorization in a tight re-election race, telling colleagues he knew the vote could cost him the seat.

What happened: Fourteen days later, on October 25, 2002 — eleven days before the election — the chartered Beechcraft King Air A100 (N41BE) carrying Wellstone crashed in woods about two miles short of the runway at Eveleth, Minnesota. All eight aboard died: the Senator, his wife Sheila, their daughter Marcia, three campaign staff, and two pilots.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The NTSB investigated for over a year with a 2,300-page factual docket. Its final report (NTSB/AAR-03/03, adopted November 18, 2003) found both engines operating normally, no pre-impact mechanical failure, negative toxicology for both pilots — and a crew flying an approach far below required airspeed for roughly fifty seconds until the airplane stalled.
  • The NTSB expressly found that icing did not affect the airplane's performance (Finding 10), rejecting the explanation carried in early press coverage.
  • The airplane carried no cockpit voice recorder and was not required to; the Board could not determine which pilot was flying.
  • Wellstone himself recounted to colleagues that Vice President Cheney had warned him a vote against the war would bring "severe ramifications" for him and Minnesota — Wellstone's account, reported in the press at the time.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Accident. The NTSB's probable cause: "the flight crew's failure to maintain adequate airspeed, which led to an aerodynamic stall from which they did not recover." The FBI advised the NTSB within a day of the crash that there were "no indications of terrorist activity," and no criminal investigation followed.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Professor James Fetzer and Don "Four Arrows" Jacobs argued in American Assassination: The Strange Death of Senator Paul Wellstone (2004) that the crash was a directed killing — by a small bomb, a gas device, or an electromagnetic/radio-frequency weapon — and that the NTSB suppressed inquiry. Those claims are assertions by named authors; no investigative body has adopted them, and no physical evidence of a device has ever been produced.

Sources: NTSB, Aircraft Accident Report AAR-03/03 (adopted Nov 18, 2003) and Public Docket 34064 · Plane & Pilot, "The Wellstone Accident" (April 2004) · Fetzer & Jacobs, American Assassination (2004) — cited as the assertion of record.

B6. Dr. David Kelly (May 14, 1944 – July 17, 2003)

Who he was / what he said: British Ministry of Defence scientist and former UN weapons inspector in Iraq — one of the world's leading authorities on Iraq's biological-weapons program. In May 2003 he spoke off the record to BBC journalist Andrew Gilligan and questioned how the government's September 2002 dossier had presented Iraqi weapons intelligence, including the claim that weapons could be deployed in 45 minutes. When his name became public, he was hauled before Parliament's Foreign Affairs Committee on July 15, 2003.

What happened: On the afternoon of July 17, 2003, Kelly went for a walk near his Oxfordshire home and did not return. His body was found the next morning at Harrowdown Hill, his left wrist cut, an open packet of co-proxamol painkillers and a bottle of water nearby.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • No full coroner's inquest into Kelly's death has ever been held. The inquest was opened and then suspended in August 2003 under Section 17A of the Coroners Act 1988 because of the Hutton Inquiry, and was never resumed — an exceptional procedural path for an unexpected death.
  • A group of medical professionals — including trauma surgeon David Halpin, Dr. Christopher Burns-Cox, and Dr. C. Stephen Frost — formally argued in print and in legal applications (2009–2011) that the recorded cause of death, haemorrhage from a severed left ulnar artery, could not by itself account for death as described, and applied to the Attorney General for a fresh inquest.
  • Attorney General Dominic Grieve reviewed the case in 2010–2011 and declined to seek an inquest on June 9, 2011. The post-mortem and toxicology reports were released in October 2010; both supported the Hutton conclusion.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Suicide. The Hutton Inquiry (report, January 28, 2004) concluded Kelly died by his own hand: haemorrhage from incised wounds of the left wrist, combined with co-proxamol ingestion and coronary artery disease as a contributory factor, and that no other party was involved. The Attorney General in 2011 called the evidence that Kelly killed himself "overwhelmingly strong."

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Norman Baker, then a Liberal Democrat MP, investigated for over a year and argued in The Strange Death of David Kelly (2007) that Kelly was murdered. Former Conservative leader Michael Howard also stated publicly that he believed Kelly was murdered. These are assertions by named public figures; the physicians who challenged the verdict framed their case as medical and procedural questions and did not adopt a specific alternative account.

Sources: Hutton Inquiry Report (January 28, 2004) · Attorney General's statement (June 9, 2011) · Post-mortem and toxicology reports (released October 2010) · Norman Baker, The Strange Death of David Kelly (Methuen, 2007) — cited as the assertion of record · Wikipedia, "David Kelly (weapons expert)" (summary of the inquest and review record).

B7. John P. Wheeler III (December 14, 1944 – c. December 30, 2010)

Who he was / what he carried: West Point graduate and Vietnam veteran; chairman of the Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund, which built the Wall; special assistant to Secretaries of the Air Force under Presidents Reagan and George H.W. Bush; later a consultant to the MITRE Corporation working on cyber and defense matters. A man who had spent forty years inside the American defense establishment.

What happened: In the last days of December 2010, Wheeler was captured on surveillance cameras in Wilmington, Delaware, disoriented and disheveled — in one building holding a shoe, telling strangers his briefcase had been stolen. He was last seen alive on the evening of December 30, 2010. On the morning of December 31, a landfill worker at the Cherry Island Landfill in Wilmington watched a body fall from a garbage truck. It was Wheeler. The truck's load had come from commercial dumpsters in Newark, Delaware, some fourteen miles away. His West Point ring and Rolex were still on the body.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • This entry is in this dossier on the strength of the official record itself: the Delaware Division of Forensic Science announced on January 28, 2011, that Wheeler died of assault and blunt force trauma and that the manner of death was homicide (Delaware DHSS / Division of Forensic Science, January 2011).
  • More than fifteen years later, the homicide is unsolved. No suspect has been named, no arrest made, and investigators have never publicly established where Wheeler was killed, how he traveled from Wilmington to Newark, or how his body entered the dumpster (Newark Police statements, February 2011; FBI Vault file).
  • Police reported that on December 28, 2010, a man matching Wheeler's description threw smoke bombs into a neighbor's house under construction — part of an ongoing dispute over that construction (police statements, January 2011).
  • Wheeler had a documented diagnosis of bipolar disorder, which investigators and his family have cited as context for his disoriented final days.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Homicide by person or persons unknown. Authorities have stated plainly that they do not know whether Wheeler was the victim of a random attack on a disoriented man or a targeted killing.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Commentators have asserted a targeted assassination connected to his defense and cyber work. That is speculation: no evidence linking his work to his death has ever been produced, and the investigating agencies have never endorsed it.

Sources: Delaware Division of Forensic Science, cause-of-death announcement (January 28, 2011) · Newark Police Department statements (2011) · FBI Vault, John P. Wheeler III file · Wikipedia, "John P. Wheeler III" (summary of the investigation record).

B8. Michael Hastings (January 28, 1980 – June 18, 2013)

Who he was / what he wrote: Rolling Stone contributing editor and BuzzFeed reporter. His June 2010 profile "The Runaway General" forced the resignation of Gen. Stanley McChrystal, the U.S. commander in Afghanistan. His 2012 Rolling Stone story on Sgt. Bowe Bergdahl drew on private family emails. By 2013 he was an open critic of the surveillance state; his last published piece, "Why Democrats Love to Spy on Americans," appeared June 7, 2013.

What happened: At about 4:25 a.m. on June 18, 2013, Hastings' Mercedes C250 coupé, traveling at high speed down Highland Avenue in Los Angeles, ran red lights, struck a palm tree in the Hancock Park neighborhood, and burst into flames. The engine was thrown fifty to sixty yards. Hastings died of massive blunt force trauma; his body was identified by fingerprints the FBI had on file.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED:

  • The day before he died, Hastings emailed BuzzFeed colleagues that he was "onto a big story," that the FBI was interviewing his close friends and associates, and that he needed to go "off the radar" for a bit (email of June 17, 2013, published by BuzzFeed/LA Weekly after his death).   • The FBI initially denied investigating him. After journalists Jason Leopold and Ryan Shapiro sued under FOIA, the FBI released a 21-page file on Hastings on September 24, 2013, showing the Bureau's Washington field office had opened a file in June 2012 to store articles and "memorialize controversial reporting by Rolling Stone magazine." The FBI maintained he was never himself under investigation.
  • Toxicology in the coroner's report detected marijuana and amphetamine compounds.

WHAT THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT SAYS: Accident. The LAPD announced within two days that there were no signs of foul play, and the Los Angeles County Coroner ruled the death an accident caused by massive blunt force trauma consistent with a high-speed vehicular collision.

WHAT IS ASSERTED: Former U.S. counterterrorism adviser Richard Clarke said publicly in July 2013 that the crash was "consistent with a car cyberattack" and that intelligence services had demonstrated the capability to remotely control vehicles — an assertion about capability and consistency, not evidence about this crash. Other commentators have asserted Hastings was killed over the story he was pursuing. Members of Hastings' own family who were with him in his final days have publicly rejected the assassination theories — that rejection is also on the record, and this dossier prints both.

Sources: Los Angeles County Coroner report (August 2013) · LAPD statements (June 2013) · FBI file on Michael Hastings (released Sept 24, 2013, under FOIA litigation) · BuzzFeed / LA Weekly, publication of the June 17, 2013 email · Richard Clarke remarks, as reported by the Huffington Post (July 2013).

B9. Dr. Jeff Bradstreet (July 6, 1954 – June 19, 2015)

Who he was / what he promoted: Physician and former preacher who ran alternative-medicine practices treating autism, which he attributed to vaccine injury in his own son's case. By 2015 he was a leading promoter of GcMAF, an unapproved blood-derived treatment he marketed for autism and cancer — work that put him in direct conflict with the FDA. His GcMAF research was later retracted by the journals that published it.

What happened: On June 18, 2015, FDA agents and Georgia state narcotics officers executed a search warrant at Bradstreet's clinic in Buford, Georgia, in the GcMAF investigation. The next day, June 19, 2015, a fisherman found Bradstreet's body in the Rocky Broad River in Rutherford County, North Carolina, with a single gunshot wound to the chest. A gun was recovered from the river.

THE PATTERN ON THE RECORD

The entries above are cases. This closing section is about capability — and capability, unlike the cases, is fully documented. The United States government has stated, in its own records, under oath and in its own reports, that it built the instruments this dossier's subjects feared. What is documented is that the instruments exist and were used against dissent. Whether any given instrument was ever turned on any given person in this dossier is a separate question, and it must be proven case by case — that discipline is what separates this page from rumor.

1. A weapon designed to kill and leave no trace — displayed to the Senate. On September 16, 1975, CIA Director William Colby testified before the Church Committee and presented an electrically fired dart pistol developed under MKNAOMI, the CIA's joint program with the Army's biological-warfare establishment at Fort Detrick. A CIA memorandum described it as a "nondiscernible microbioinoculator." The dart was minute — about the width of a human hair — carried frozen saxitoxin (shellfish toxin), and was designed to dissolve after penetrating the skin, so that a death could be mistaken for natural cardiac or respiratory failure. Colby also confirmed the CIA had retained roughly 11 grams of shellfish toxin and cobra venom in defiance of President Nixon's 1969–70 destruction orders. No victim of this device has ever been documented; its existence has been documented since 1975. Sources: Church Committee hearings (Sept 16, 1975) and Final Report, Book I (April 1976); New York Times, Sept 17, 1975.

2. A program of experiments on unwitting citizens — with its files deliberately burned. MKULTRA, approved by Director Allen Dulles on April 13, 1953, ran from 1953 to about 1973 across roughly 150 subprojects at some 80 institutions — universities, hospitals, prisons — administering LSD and other drugs to subjects who did not know they were being dosed. Frank Olson (entry B3) died inside it. Director Richard Helms ordered the program's files destroyed in 1973; the record that exists survives because some 20,000 financial documents were misfiled and found in 1977. Sidney Gottlieb, who ran the program, himself described the research as largely unproductive — the documented crime is the experimentation and the cover-up, not successful mind control. Sources: Church Committee (1975–76); Rockefeller Commission (1975); Joint Senate hearing, August 3, 1977 (testimony of Director Stansfield Turner); further releases via the National Security Archive (December 2024).

3. A domestic program to "neutralize" dissent — documented down to the letter. COINTELPRO, the FBI's counterintelligence programs (1956–1971), targeted domestic political movements for exposure, disruption, and — in the Bureau's own word — "neutralization." The program was exposed not by any inquiry but by citizens who burgled the FBI's Media, Pennsylvania office on March 8, 1971, and mailed the files to newspapers. The Church Committee then documented the program's scope, including the FBI's anonymous 1964 letter to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. — sent with a tape of surveillance material — that King and his aides understood as urging him to kill himself before accepting the Nobel Peace Prize. That letter is in the record; its full text was released from the National Archives in 2014. Sources: Church Committee Final Report, Books II–III (1976); FBI COINTELPRO files (released under FOIA); New York Times, publication of the full King letter (November 2014).
